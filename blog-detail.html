How Mercury retrograde really moves you

May 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Beyond the memes — a Vedic reading of what to release, and what to reweave.

There is a quiet doorway in classical Vedic astrology that modern conversations often skip past. It does not begin with Sun signs, nor even with planets. It begins with the Moon — and where she happens to be standing at the moment of your birth.

The ancients mapped the sky not in twelve, but in twenty-seven slices, each governed by a presiding deity, a symbol, a sound, a temperament. They called these the Nakshatras — lunar mansions, the rooms in which the Moon rests as she travels.

A different way of seeing

Where the western chart describes a personality, the Nakshatra describes a texture — a mood, a karmic inclination, a particular way the soul learns. Two people with the same Sun sign can hold wildly different Nakshatras, and feel, to themselves, like entirely different beings.

"The Moon does not change the sky, but she changes the way the sky feels."

In the chapters that follow, we'll walk through a handful of Nakshatras — not to label, but to listen. What each one is whispering, when it shows up in your chart.
